Privacy Policy — Veer

App: Veer: Punjabi Senior Helper — for iPhone, iPad and Android

Provided by: Logicstack LLC

Effective date: 10 September 2026

Contact: kernelkrafttech@gmail.com

Veer is a simple companion app designed for older Punjabi-speaking adults, to help with medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts, prayer (Gurbani), news and everyday tasks. We built Veer to be private by default. This policy explains, in plain terms, what Veer handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. It applies to the app on both Apple and Android devices; where the two platforms differ, that is spelled out.

The short version: Veer has no account and no login. Your medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts, notes and health details are stored on your own device and are never sent to us. We operate no servers that hold your data, and there are no ads and no advertising trackers. Some features must contact outside services to work at all — weather, news and prayer audio, address suggestions, turning speech into text, and the optional AI helper. Those are listed in section 4, and the AI helper is switched off until you add your own key, read what is sent, and agree.

1. We do not collect your data on our servers

Veer has no user accounts, no sign-in and no servers that store your information. Everything you enter stays in the app's private storage on your phone. We, the developer, cannot see it, cannot retrieve it and cannot restore it. Uninstalling the app permanently removes this data from your device.

2. Information stored on your device

The following is saved locally on your phone. Veer never uploads any of it to us, and none of it is ever sent to an AI company as stored data:

  • Profile details you choose to enter — your name, age, emergency contact, allergies and conditions
  • Medicines, dosages, dose times and which doses you have marked as taken
  • Doctors and health-care contacts
  • Appointments and to-do items
  • Family and emergency contacts — the names and phone numbers you type in yourself
  • Personal notes
  • Your settings: language, text size, theme, accessibility preferences and your emergency number
  • Your AI provider key, if you choose to add one
  • Your answer to the AI permission screen — which company you agreed to, and when

Veer does not read your device's own contact list, call history, text messages, photos library or files. Contacts inside Veer are only the ones you type in yourself.

Two honest exceptions. Some of what is in that list can leave your phone as part of something you actively do, rather than as stored data:
  • An address you type into a doctor or an appointment is sent to an address service so it can suggest real places. You have to pick one of its suggestions to save the address. See section 4.
  • Words you speak or type into "Ask Veer" are sent to an AI company, but only if you have added your own key and agreed on the permission screen. If you add a family member by voice, the sentence you speak carries their name and phone number, so those words go too. This is spelled out on the permission screen before you agree, and again in section 4.
Neither of these ever sends your saved lists. The difference is between data Veer holds and words you choose to say.

4. Information sent to outside services

Some features need the internet. In each case only the minimum needed for that feature is sent, always over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection, and never your stored personal or health information.

About every company named in this section. Before sending anything to a third party we read that company's published privacy terms and satisfy ourselves that they provide protection equivalent to what this policy describes: they receive only what the feature needs, they act on the request rather than building a profile of you, and they apply security measures to data in transit and at rest. One provider does not meet that bar on one of its tiers — Google's free Gemini tier — and rather than drop the disclosure into this page alone, Veer states the difference on the permission screen before you choose it. The per-provider detail is under "Optional AI assistance" below. We do not sell your personal information to anyone, and none of these companies is an advertising network or a data broker. Some are public bodies (the US Census Bureau) or non-profit and open-data projects (OpenStreetMap, Open-Meteo, the Internet Archive), and some are the makers of your phone's own operating system (Apple, Google), acting under the privacy commitments they publish for those platform services. We link to those policies where the feature that uses them is described, so you can read what each commits to.

Weather and location

If you allow location access, Veer sends your coordinates to Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) to fetch the forecast, and to BigDataCloud (api.bigdatacloud.net) to turn those coordinates into a place name to show you.

If you allow location but the phone cannot get a satellite fix — location services switched off system-wide, or indoors where the fix times out — Veer falls back to estimating your rough area from your internet connection, using ipapi.co or ipwho.is, which receive your device's IP address.

If you decline location access, Veer shows no weather at all. It does not fall back to your internet connection in that case, because you have said no and estimating your town anyway would go against that. Veer will ask again the next time you open it. Your location is used only for weather, is not kept as a history, and is never used for advertising or tracking.

Voice (microphone)

When you use the "Ask Veer" voice feature, your speech is turned into text by your phone's own speech recogniser. Veer itself never records, stores or uploads your audio — only the resulting text is used, and that text goes to an AI company only if you have added your own key, turned the option on, and agreed on the permission screen (see below). Otherwise it never leaves your phone.

Please note that the recogniser belongs to your phone, not to Veer, and it may perform the transcription on its own servers: on iPhone and iPad, Apple may send the audio to Apple to transcribe it, and on Android, Google may do the same. That step is handled under Apple's and Google's own privacy policies. You can avoid it entirely by typing your question instead of speaking it.

Scan a label (camera)

When you photograph a medicine label or prescription to fill in a form, the text is read on your device using offline recognition, and the photo stays on your phone. The temporary image file is deleted as soon as it has been read.

There is one exception, and Veer always asks first. If you have added your own AI key and switched on "smarter understanding", Veer can offer to send that one photo to the AI provider you chose, which reads labels far more accurately. Veer asks your permission every single time, names the provider in the question, and reads the photo on your phone instead if you say no. No image ever leaves your device without you agreeing to it on that occasion.

Optional AI assistance (bring your own key)

Veer can optionally use a third-party AI company to understand your questions more flexibly. It is off until you do three things: add your own API key in Settings, turn on "Send my questions to an AI company", and then read a full-screen permission notice and tap Yes. Until all three have happened, Veer sends nothing to any AI company, and it answers every question from what is saved on your phone.

What is sent. The words of your question — what you speak, or what you type. That is the whole of it, with one important addition: if you add a medicine, doctor, appointment or family member by voice, the sentence you speak is sent as well, because that is how Veer fills in the form for you. So if you say "add my son Manpreet, five five five one two three four", that name and that phone number are in what is sent. The permission screen says this in those words before you agree.

What is never sent. The medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts and notes already saved in Veer. Your name, your age, your allergies and conditions. Your location. None of that is included in an AI request, ever.

Who it is sent to — determined by the key you enter, and named on screen before anything is sent:

  • OpenAI, at api.openai.com — privacy policy
  • Google (Gemini), at generativelanguage.googleapis.com — privacy policy
  • Anthropic (Claude), at api.anthropic.com — privacy policy

Protection by these companies. Before supporting a provider we read its published privacy and API terms, and we tell you what we found rather than assuring you in general terms. As of this policy's date:

  • OpenAI and Anthropic, via their business API terms: content submitted through the API is not used to train their general models, it is processed to carry out the request, and it is protected by security measures in transit and at rest. Both retain API content for a limited period for abuse monitoring. This matches the protection this policy describes.
  • Google (Gemini): the paid API tier carries comparable commitments. The free tier does not — Google states that human reviewers may read content sent on the free tier, and that it may be used to improve Google's products. This is weaker than what this policy describes, and we do not want that difference buried in a policy page.

We still support Google keys, because many people already have one and the choice of provider is yours to make. What we do about the difference is disclose it where the decision is actually taken: when your key is a Google one, Veer says this on the permission screen, in the same words, before you tap Yes. If we ever add a provider whose terms are weaker than this policy describes, we will tell you the same way — at the point of choosing, not only here.

Stopping it. Open Settings and either turn off "Send my questions to an AI company" or tap "Turn off AI help and delete my key". Either one stops every AI request immediately; the second also erases the key from your phone. You can re-read the whole permission notice at any time, without a key, from Settings or from the "Ask Veer" screen.

AI answers are generated by a machine and can be wrong. Veer is instructed to decline medical questions and refer you to your doctor, but you should never rely on an AI answer for a health decision. Every AI answer in the app is labelled with the company that helped produce it, and carries a "Report this answer" link that emails it to us.

Address search

When you type a place into an appointment or a doctor's address, what you type is sent to open address-lookup services so they can suggest matching addresses: Photon / OpenStreetMap (photon.komoot.io, operated by komoot GmbH) and, for United States addresses, the US Census Bureau geocoder (geocoding.geo.census.gov). Only the text of the address is sent — your location is not, and nothing else about you is included.

Please note that these address fields require you to pick one of the suggestions before the address can be saved, so this lookup is part of saving an address rather than something you can skip. The app says so underneath the field. Photon is a free service run by komoot GmbH under their privacy policy; the US Census geocoder is a United States federal government service. Neither receives anything that identifies you, and neither is sent your saved lists.

Directions

Tapping "Directions" on an appointment or a doctor hands the address you saved to Google Maps in your browser or maps app, so it can route you there. Only that one address is passed across. From that point you are in Google Maps rather than in Veer, under Google's privacy policy — we link it here so you know before you tap.

App configuration

When Veer starts, it downloads a small public file (kernelkrafttech-hue.github.io) listing the web addresses of the radio and prayer streams the app offers. This lets us replace a station that has gone off the air without making you wait for an app update. Nothing about you is sent with that request — no name, no settings, no identifier; it is the same file for everyone. As with any web request, it reveals your device's IP address to GitHub, which hosts the file. The file can only substitute audio addresses: it cannot change any of your settings, turn a feature on or off, or add anything to the app.

News, prayer and live audio

The News, Gurbani and live radio features fetch publicly available content from third parties, including Google News, BBC Punjabi (feeds.bbci.co.uk), the Internet Archive (archive.org), SikhNet (radio.sikhnet.com), SGPC (live.sgpc.net) and All India Radio. These are ordinary content requests — no personal information is attached to them. One thing is worth saying plainly, because it is not obvious: like any request to a website, connecting to one of these streams reveals your device's IP address to whoever runs it. For a prayer or religious broadcast that discloses something about you to that broadcaster, even though Veer attaches nothing. Veer does not save this audio to your phone. Opening a news article or a TV channel hands you over to your browser or to YouTube, which have their own privacy policies.

5. Emergency calls

The Emergency screen can dial your country's emergency-services number, or a different number you set yourself in Settings, and can call a family contact you have saved. Veer places the call through your phone's own dialler and keeps no record of it. We receive nothing when you use this feature.

Veer is not an emergency service. It only dials a number for you. It does not contact emergency services on your behalf, does not monitor you, and cannot summon help by itself. On iPhone and iPad your phone will always ask you to confirm before the call is placed.

6. Notifications

Notifications are created and scheduled entirely on your device, by the app itself. They are used only to remind you about the medicines, appointments and tasks that you entered. Veer sends no push notifications from any server — there is no server — and nothing about your reminders is transmitted anywhere.

7. No ads, analytics or tracking

Veer contains no advertising, no advertising trackers, no analytics product of ours, and no tracking software. Veer does not use an advertising identifier, does not track you across other apps or websites, and does not build a profile of you. We do not sell or share your personal information, and we never have.

9. Children

Veer is designed for adults, particularly older adults, and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has provided information through this app, please contact us and we will help you remove it — though in practice, all such information is on the device itself and can be deleted there.

10. Security and retention

Data on your device is protected by your phone's standard app sandboxing, which prevents other apps from reading it, and by your device passcode. Everything sent to the outside services described above travels over encrypted HTTPS connections. Because your personal data lives only on your device, we retain none of it; it is kept until you delete it in the app or uninstall Veer.

Veer's data is excluded from your phone's cloud backup. On Android the app declares allowBackup="false", and also refuses device-to-device transfer. On iOS the data file is marked NSURLIsExcludedFromBackupKey. So your medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts, notes, health profile, your AI key and your recorded answer to the permission screen are not copied to iCloud or to Google Drive.

This has a cost, and we would rather you knew it now than found out later: if you replace, lose or erase your phone, this information cannot be restored — a new phone starts empty and you will need to enter it again. We chose that because the file holds health information, family phone numbers and an API key in plain text, and we would rather it never left your device at all. Veer says the same thing in Settings, under "Your information".

11. Your choices and your rights

  • You can decline, or later revoke, any permission — location, microphone, speech, camera, photos, notifications — in your phone's settings, at any time.
  • You can delete any individual item (a medicine, a contact, a note) inside the app.
  • You can withdraw your permission for the AI helper at any time, in Settings: turn off "Send my questions to an AI company", or tap "Turn off AI help and delete my key", which also erases the key from your phone. Either one stops every AI request immediately.
  • You can re-read the full AI permission notice at any time — with or without a key — from Settings or from the "Ask Veer" screen.
  • You can set or change the emergency number Veer dials in Settings.
  • Uninstalling Veer permanently removes everything the app stored on your device.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, California or another region with data protection rights — such as the right to access, correct, delete or export your personal data, or to object to its processing — those rights apply to personal data a company holds about you. Because Logicstack LLC holds none of your personal data on any server, there is nothing for us to retrieve, correct or erase: you already hold and control all of it on your own device, and deleting it there is final. If you have a question about this, please write to us at the address below and we will help.

12. Differences between iPhone and Android

  • My apps: available on Android only. Apple does not allow an app to see or open other apps, so this feature is not present on iPhone or iPad.
  • Screen off: available on Android only, using a permission you grant once. Apple provides no equivalent, so the button is not shown on iPhone or iPad.
  • Emergency calls: on Android, with your permission, Veer can place the call the instant you tap. On iPhone and iPad your phone always shows its own confirmation first — no app can bypass that.
  • Punjabi scanning and speech: the languages available for reading Gurmukhi from a photo, and for speaking answers aloud, depend on what your phone provides. They differ between Apple and Android devices, and between models.
