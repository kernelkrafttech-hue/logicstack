Privacy Policy — Veer

App: Veer: Punjabi Senior Helper — for iPhone, iPad and Android

Provided by: Logicstack LLC

Effective date: 21 August 2026

Contact: kernelkrafttech@gmail.com

Veer is a simple companion app designed for older Punjabi-speaking adults, to help with medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts, prayer (Gurbani), news and everyday tasks. We built Veer to be private by default. This policy explains, in plain terms, what Veer handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. It applies to the app on both Apple and Android devices; where the two platforms differ, that is spelled out.

The short version: Veer has no account and no login. Your personal information — medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts, notes and health details — is stored only on your own device and is never sent to us or to anyone else. We operate no servers that hold your data. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking of any kind. A few features (weather, news, prayer audio and an optional AI helper) contact outside services, and only for the reasons described below.

1. We do not collect your data on our servers

Veer has no user accounts, no sign-in and no servers that store your information. Everything you enter stays in the app's private storage on your phone. We, the developer, cannot see it, cannot retrieve it and cannot restore it. Uninstalling the app permanently removes this data from your device.

2. Information stored on your device only

The following is saved locally on your phone and is never transmitted off the device:

  • Profile details you choose to enter — your name, age, emergency contact, allergies and conditions
  • Medicines, dosages, dose times and which doses you have marked as taken
  • Doctors and health-care contacts
  • Appointments and to-do items
  • Family and emergency contacts — the names and phone numbers you type in yourself
  • Personal notes
  • Your settings: language, text size, theme, accessibility preferences and your emergency number
  • Your AI provider key, if you choose to add one

Veer does not read your device's own contact list, call history, text messages, photos library or files. Contacts inside Veer are only the ones you type in yourself.

4. Information sent to outside services

Some features need the internet. In each case only the minimum needed for that feature is sent, always over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection, and never your stored personal or health information.

Weather and location

If you allow location access, Veer sends your coordinates to Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) to fetch the forecast, and to BigDataCloud (api.bigdatacloud.net) to turn those coordinates into a place name to show you.

If you decline location access, Veer can instead estimate your rough area from your internet connection using ipapi.co or ipwho.is, which receive your device's IP address. Your location is used only for weather, is not kept as a history, and is never used for advertising or tracking.

Voice (microphone)

When you use the "Ask Veer" voice feature, your speech is turned into text by your phone's own speech recogniser. Veer itself never records, stores or uploads your audio — only the resulting text is used, and it is passed on to an AI service only if you have switched that option on (see below).

Please note that the recogniser belongs to your phone, not to Veer, and it may perform the transcription on its own servers: on iPhone and iPad, Apple may send the audio to Apple to transcribe it, and on Android, Google may do the same. That step is handled under Apple's and Google's own privacy policies. You can avoid it entirely by typing your question instead of speaking it.

Scan a label (camera)

When you photograph a medicine label or prescription to fill in a form, the text is read on your device using offline recognition, and the photo stays on your phone. The temporary image file is deleted as soon as it has been read.

There is one exception, and Veer always asks first. If you have added your own AI key and switched on "smarter understanding", Veer can offer to send that one photo to the AI provider you chose, which reads labels far more accurately. Veer asks your permission every single time, names the provider in the question, and reads the photo on your phone instead if you say no. No image ever leaves your device without you agreeing to it on that occasion.

Optional AI assistance (bring your own key)

Veer can optionally use a third-party AI service to understand your questions more flexibly. This is switched off unless you add your own API key in Settings and turn on "smarter understanding". When it is on, what is sent is the text of your question — and, only with your per-photo agreement described above, a photo you scanned. It goes to whichever provider your key belongs to:

  • OpenAI — privacy policy
  • Google (Gemini) — privacy policy
  • Anthropic (Claude) — privacy policy

Your saved medicines, doctors, appointments, family contacts and notes are never included in these requests. Your API key is stored only on your device, and you can delete it in Settings at any time to stop all AI requests immediately. Because you are using your own key, your use of that provider is governed by your agreement with them and by their privacy policy, not ours.

AI answers are generated by a machine and can be wrong. Veer is instructed to decline medical questions and refer you to your doctor, but you should never rely on an AI answer for a health decision. Every AI answer in the app carries a "Report this answer" link that emails it to us.

Address search

When you type a place into an appointment or a doctor's address, what you type — and, if available, your approximate coordinates — is sent to open address-lookup services to suggest matching addresses: Photon / OpenStreetMap (photon.komoot.io) and, for United States addresses, the US Census Bureau geocoder (geocoding.geo.census.gov). Nothing else about you is included.

Directions

Tapping "Directions" on an appointment or a doctor hands the address you saved to Google Maps in your browser or maps app, so it can route you there. Only that one address is passed across, and what happens next is governed by Google's privacy policy.

News, prayer and live audio

The News, Gurbani and live radio features fetch publicly available content from third parties, including Google News, BBC Punjabi (feeds.bbci.co.uk), the Internet Archive (archive.org), SikhNet (radio.sikhnet.com), SGPC (live.sgpc.net) and All India Radio. These are ordinary content requests — no personal information is attached to them. Veer does not save this audio to your phone. Opening a news article or a TV channel hands you over to your browser or to YouTube, which have their own privacy policies.

5. Emergency calls

The Emergency screen can dial your country's emergency-services number, or a different number you set yourself in Settings, and can call a family contact you have saved. Veer places the call through your phone's own dialler and keeps no record of it. We receive nothing when you use this feature.

Veer is not an emergency service. It only dials a number for you. It does not contact emergency services on your behalf, does not monitor you, and cannot summon help by itself. On iPhone and iPad your phone will always ask you to confirm before the call is placed.

6. Notifications

Notifications are created and scheduled entirely on your device, by the app itself. They are used only to remind you about the medicines, appointments and tasks that you entered. Veer sends no push notifications from any server — there is no server — and nothing about your reminders is transmitted anywhere.

7. No ads, analytics or tracking

Veer contains no advertising, no third-party analytics and no tracking software. Veer does not use an advertising identifier, does not track you across other apps or websites, and does not build a profile of you. We do not sell or share your personal information, and we never have.

9. Children

Veer is designed for adults, particularly older adults, and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has provided information through this app, please contact us and we will help you remove it — though in practice, all such information is on the device itself and can be deleted there.

10. Security and retention

Data on your device is protected by your phone's standard app sandboxing, which prevents other apps from reading it, and by your device passcode. Everything sent to the outside services described above travels over encrypted HTTPS connections. Because your personal data lives only on your device, we retain none of it; it is kept until you delete it in the app or uninstall Veer.

Note that if your phone is backed up to iCloud or to Google, your Veer data may be included in that backup, under Apple's or Google's own terms. You can exclude Veer from backups in your device settings.

11. Your choices and your rights

  • You can decline, or later revoke, any permission — location, microphone, speech, camera, photos, notifications — in your phone's settings, at any time.
  • You can delete any individual item (a medicine, a contact, a note) inside the app.
  • You can remove your AI provider key in Settings, which stops every AI request immediately.
  • You can set or change the emergency number Veer dials in Settings.
  • Uninstalling Veer permanently removes everything the app stored on your device.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, California or another region with data protection rights — such as the right to access, correct, delete or export your personal data, or to object to its processing — those rights apply to personal data a company holds about you. Because Logicstack LLC holds none of your personal data on any server, there is nothing for us to retrieve, correct or erase: you already hold and control all of it on your own device, and deleting it there is final. If you have a question about this, please write to us at the address below and we will help.

12. Differences between iPhone and Android

  • My apps: available on Android only. Apple does not allow an app to see or open other apps, so this feature is not present on iPhone or iPad.
  • Screen off: available on Android only, using a permission you grant once. Apple provides no equivalent, so the button is not shown on iPhone or iPad.
  • Emergency calls: on Android, with your permission, Veer can place the call the instant you tap. On iPhone and iPad your phone always shows its own confirmation first — no app can bypass that.
  • Punjabi scanning and speech: the languages available for reading Gurmukhi from a photo, and for speaking answers aloud, depend on what your phone provides. They differ between Apple and Android devices, and between models.
